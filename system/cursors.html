Cursors

Ten drawings on one idea, a reading desk. Eight states in use, one drawn and switched off, one cut.

Hover the hatched strip in any card to try that cursor for real, and hold the mouse down on one to see the pressed frame. Each is shown big so you can tell what it is, and at 32 pixels on both backgrounds, which is the only size it is ever actually used at. 1

One side effect worth naming. Setting default on body means paragraphs get the bookmark rather than the I beam a browser would normally show over selectable text. 7

Three rules the drawings had to obey

32 by 32, and not a pixel more. Browsers cap cursors there and ignore anything bigger instead of scaling it down, so a 64 pixel drawing does not appear at all. Drawing at final size kills any detail thinner than about two pixels. The open book lost its page curl and the bookmark lost its stitching, and both got better.

Pale body, dark outline. The curator side has a near black nav and the public side is cream. My first bookmark was solid ink and vanished into that bar, leaving only its halo behind. Every glyph is now drawn twice, a fat white stroke underneath and the real thing on top. That is why system cursors have looked like this since 1984. 8

They cannot animate. A cursor image is rasterised once and anything moving inside it is ignored, so a flipping page had to become two frames the user drives. Progress had the same problem and became a pile of books that are simply not shelved yet.

Safari

Safari ignores SVG cursors completely. Every declaration names an ordinary keyword after the file, so Safari falls back to its own arrow, hand and I beam and nothing breaks. 9
